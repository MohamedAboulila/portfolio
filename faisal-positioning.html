Positioning a brokerage around claims it could prove

Nine competitors, a pile of familiar claims, and one question: what can Faisal say that is both different and true?

01 / The situation

Wallpaper or liability.

Brokerages promote similar features. Say what everyone says and you are wallpaper; say what you cannot prove and, in a regulated market, you are a liability. The review covered Thndr, EFG Hermes, Mubasher Trade, CI Capital, Al Ahly Pharos, Beltone, Arabeya Online, Tycoon and Ostool.

The play

01Map the fieldReviewed the competitive set, then tested each proposed message against the actual product and competitor offers.
02Table stakesClassified index breadth across EGX30, EGX70 and EGX33 as a category feature, not a unique advantage.
03Scoped claimsDescribed Sharia compliance only in relation to the margin-financing product, where it is accurate.
04Honest contextNoted that account officers are common among EGX brokers except Thndr, and framed the point with that context instead of as a market-wide exclusive.

Three substantiated differentiators

01Institutional heritageFaisal Islamic Bank’s 40+ year history
02Experienced account officers7+ years’ experience; they advise, while the client approves each trade
03Concierge onboardingA representative visits the client, so an office visit is not required
02 / Claims guardrails

Keep the organization and the offer distinct.

Messaging separated Faisal Islamic Bank’s heritage from FBC Brokerage’s own establishment history, with accuracy rules written for Arabic copy.

03 / The output

A practical reference for brand communication.

The positioning brief gave website, social, and campaign copy a documented basis for describing the service accurately, including guidance for Arabic-language messaging.

The lesson

A claim everyone makes is not a differentiator, and a claim you cannot prove is a loan against your credibility. Positioning is the discipline of saying only what survives both tests.

Steal this play

List your top five marketing claims. Strike every one a competitor also makes. Then ask, about each survivor: could we prove this in front of a regulator? Keep what passes.
