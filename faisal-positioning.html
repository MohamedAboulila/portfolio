Positioning a brokerage around claims it could prove

A competitor review separated category basics from the differentiators the brokerage could credibly own in a regulated market.

01 / The challenge

In a regulated category, a familiar claim may not be a differentiator.

Brokerages often promote similar features. Mohamed needed to understand which claims were common across the market and which could help Faisal Securities stand apart without overstating what the service offered.

02 / The competitive review

Map the field, then test each claim against reality.

The review covered Thndr, EFG Hermes, Mubasher Trade, CI Capital, Al Ahly Pharos, Beltone, Arabeya Online, Tycoon, and Ostool. Proposed messages were checked against the actual product and competitor offer.

Index breadth across EGX30, EGX70, and EGX33 was treated as a category feature rather than a unique advantage. Sharia compliance was described only in relation to the margin-financing product.

Three substantiated differentiators

01Institutional heritageFaisal Islamic Bank’s 40+ year history
02Experienced account officers7+ years’ experience; they advise, while the client approves each trade
03Concierge onboardingA representative visits the client, so an office visit is not required
03 / Claims guardrails

Keep the organization and the offer distinct.

Messaging separated Faisal Islamic Bank’s heritage from FBC Brokerage’s own establishment history. The review noted that account officers are common among EGX brokers except Thndr, so that point was used with the right context instead of presented as a market-wide exclusive.

04 / The output

A practical reference for brand communication.

The positioning brief gave website, social, and campaign copy a documented basis for describing the service accurately, including guidance for Arabic-language messaging.

Scope

Competitive set review, message testing, differentiator definition, and claims guidance for Arabic copy.
