Building a 24-month omnichannel growth plan

01 / The strategic question

How can a trusted retailer grow beyond its seasonal peak?

Bakier’s business has two demand engines: consumer retail and business-to-business supply. The strategy treated the combination as a strength. Back-to-School can drive high seasonal volume; corporate accounts can create recurring, less seasonal revenue; and an omnichannel customer identity can support retention across stores, website, app, and marketplaces.

02 / The diagnosis

The issue was disconnected channels, not missing channels.

The report assessed retail and B2B models, customer segments, buying behavior, seasonality, competition, digital channels, and the customer journey. It identified underused social content, search visibility, and CRM as opportunities, alongside the challenge of fragmented customer identities across channels.

03 / The roadmap

Prove first, then scale.

The first 90 days establish baselines, validate assumptions with Finance and customer research, activate customer capture, improve high-intent search, and lock the Back-to-School plan. Later phases launch B2B outreach, loyalty and CRM flows, seasonal campaigns, and a wider partner program.

0–3 moProveAudit, baseline, quick wins
3–6 moLaunchSeason and B2B motion
6–12 moScaleLoyalty and retention
12–24 moCompoundExpand channels and reach
04 / Proposed scorecard

Make assumptions measurable.

The proposal set out planning targets including a 22% to 30% repeat-purchase rate, doubling digital sales share, 120,000 CRM profiles, and a 4× blended return on marketing investment. The scorecard translated the proposal into measurable planning targets for repeat purchase, digital sales, CRM growth, and marketing return.
