Case study 05 · Marketing growth proposal

Building a 24-month omnichannel growth plan

A strategic growth proposal for a Cairo retailer and distributor with two demand engines: seasonal retail peaks and recurring corporate accounts. This was a proposal, so every target here is a planning target, clearly labeled.

01 / The strategic question

How can a trusted retailer grow beyond its seasonal peak?

Back-to-School drives high seasonal volume. Corporate accounts can create recurring, less seasonal revenue. The strategy treated the combination as a strength: let the season feed the relationship, and the relationship fund the next season.

02 / The diagnosis

The issue was disconnected channels, not missing channels.

The assessment covered retail and B2B models, customer segments, buying behavior, seasonality, competition, digital channels and the customer journey. The opportunities: underused social content, search visibility and CRM, plus customer identities fragmented across stores, website, app and marketplaces.

03 / The roadmap

Prove first, then scale.

The first 90 days establish baselines, validate assumptions with Finance and customer research, activate customer capture, improve high-intent search, and lock the Back-to-School plan. Later phases launch B2B outreach, loyalty and CRM flows, seasonal campaigns, and a wider partner program.

04 / Proposed scorecard

Make assumptions measurable.

The proposal set out planning targets, not promises: a 22% to 30% repeat-purchase rate, doubling digital sales share, 120,000 CRM profiles, and a 4× blended return on marketing investment, with an assumptions register so every target could be checked.

The lesson

A good growth plan does not invent demand. It finds the two engines a business already has and designs one to feed the other.

Steal this play

Name your two demand engines, the seasonal one and the recurring one. Then ask what would make the first feed the second. That answer is your strategy’s first page.
