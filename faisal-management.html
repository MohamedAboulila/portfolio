Making a new marketing function visible to leadership

A function that cannot explain itself does not get funded. This is how a scattered remit became one accountable story leadership could act on.

01 / The situation

Several workstreams, no shared story.

When I joined, digital activity, brand materials, website work, lead generation, social strategy and traditional media all needed one owner and one shared direction. Leadership also needed to see how the pieces fit the business, not a list of channels.

The play

01The remitDocumented the scope end to end: PPC, SEO, website redesign, lead-generation pipelines, social strategy, brand materials and TV media partnerships.
02One systemConnected every workstream to the brokerage’s human-led account-opening process, so visibility and demand generation read as one system.
03Report, then deckWrote the report in Arabic, then rebuilt it as a branded presentation in Faisal’s visual identity when management asked for the deck.
04On the recordPresented the function to upper management, and wrote the role’s LinkedIn job description so the scope was documented outside the building too.
02 / The result

A remit leadership could act on.

The report, the deck and the role description gave stakeholders one concise view of what marketing covers and how it connects. The same function carried the reported campaign results: 1,000 leads in five days and a 10% increase in trading account openings.

The lesson

Documentation is not admin. The one-page account of what your function owns, and why, is often the difference between a budget and a shrug.

Steal this play

Write the one-page version of what you own before anyone asks. If you cannot produce it in an afternoon, that is the finding.
